That happens. And there are two reasons why.

First, the system was probably undersized — it doesn't produce enough energy to cover their usage, so they're still pulling from the grid every month. Sunrun's program is designed to produce 100–107% of your estimated electricity usage. The system proposed for your home produces more energy than you currently use, specifically so you're not in that situation.

Second, your friend's system may not have a battery. Under California's current net billing rules — NEM 3.0 — the grid pays almost nothing for excess solar you send back. The credit dropped by about 75% compared to the old program. Without a battery, your friend is sending cheap power to Edison during the day and buying expensive power back at night during peak hours at 58–74¢ per kWh.

Your system includes a Tesla Powerwall. Instead of giving excess power to Edison for pennies, the battery stores it and your home uses it during peak hours — when Edison charges the most. That's why your Edison bill drops to the minimum connection fee. Not because of net metering credits, but because you're barely touching the grid at all.
